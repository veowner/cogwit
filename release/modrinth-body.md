![An arrow flies at the player's back and a stone slab tears out of the ground to catch it]({{slab}})

Cogwit is a little copper robot who sits in your charm slot and rides on your shoulder. You feed him copper. In return he learns from every kind of damage you take and starts taking part of it off, and he keeps an eye on what you don't see coming, like the creeper behind you or the lava on the other side of the block you're mining.

It started as a helper for a co-op world I play with a friend, and it's still an alpha: the two of us are the only people who have played it so far. Expect things to break.

## How he works

Shift + right click with copper to feed him, or click copper onto him in your inventory. Raw copper is worth 15 tokens, an ingot 30 and a block 270, and he holds up to 2000.

The first time something hurts you in a new way, say fire or a skeleton's arrows, he only takes notes. From then on he blocks part of that damage every time, for 4 tokens per point. Each kind of damage is its own skill and gets better the more he blocks it, topping out at 40% unless the server changes that. Poison and other bad effects he can shrug off for 5 tokens. Damage from other players is the one thing he leaves alone.

If a hit would kill you, he takes it instead. You keep 2 HP and get three seconds where nothing can touch you, and he goes to sleep for ten minutes. A Copper Apple (eight ingots around an apple) wakes him after three. Running out of tokens also puts him to sleep, for five minutes, and a sleeping Cogwit blocks nothing.

He levels up as he goes: 1 experience per point of damage blocked, 25 for saving your life and 200 when a boss dies near you.

| Level | Experience | Opens |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 50 | Brackets on threats and arrows pointing at the ones behind you; mobs hunting you glow |
| 2 | 200 | Valuable drops glow; the ore scan |
| 3 | 500 | The back shield, once every 8 seconds for 25 tokens |
| 4 | 1000 | His three brothers |
| 5 | 2000 | Overdrive in boss fights; the back shield turns to copper |

## What he notices

![Brackets and labels over hostile mobs: orange ones are coming at you, red ones are about to hit]({{threats}})

Any hostile mob you can see within 16 blocks gets a bracket and a label. Orange means it's coming for you or drawing a bow; red means a creeper is hissing or something is already in the air. When the threat is behind you, an arrow shows up next to the crosshair. Bosses get marked from 48 blocks.

He doesn't talk in chat. What he's thinking appears as one short line above the hotbar: "Lava behind this block." "Drop ahead, 14 blocks." When mobs box you in, it's "Surrounded, 5 of them. Back off left, it's clear." He'll also mention it at dusk if you have no bed, and when your air or an enchanted tool is about to run out.

![A ring rolls out from the player and ores light up through the ground as it reaches them]({{scan}})

From level 2 you can ask him to scan. A wave rolls out 32 blocks and ores light up through the ground as it passes, each kind in its own color, modded ores too. Chests and other containers show up as well. The scan has a 30 second cooldown, so it works as a quick look around rather than a permanent x-ray.

## What he does on his own

![The agent screen: every action can be set to ask, always or never]({{agent}})

None of this happens unless you allow it. Every action can be set to ask, always or never, and there are presets for careful play, fighting and mining. When he asks, Y is yes and Shift + Y is always.

Food is the simple part: he feeds you when you're hungry and doesn't touch your golden apples. Weapons take more thought. He weighs enchantments like Smite against the mob in front of you and looks at its armor too, because a slow heavy hit gets through armor better than a quick light one.

When you're close to dying he can throw an ender pearl for you. He aims for the side with fewer enemies and solid ground, and checks it for lava and drops first. Armor comes off and enchanted tools get put away a couple of uses before they would break. Hotbar slots you've emptied get refilled from your inventory, backpacks and shulker boxes.

He also picks up how you fight. If you mostly use a bow, he hands it to you at closer range and leans on defensive moves. If you fight up close, he goes for dashes and hits that sweep everything around you.

## The back shield

That's the GIF at the top. From level 3, when something hits you from behind, a slab tears out of the ground and takes the blow, and arrows aimed at your back get caught before they land. The slab is stone, sandstone or packed ice depending on what you're standing on, and copper once he reaches level 5.

## His brothers

![Spark, Rivet and Anvil jump down from the player's shoulder]({{crew}})

At level 4 Spark, Rivet and Anvil start jumping down to fight next to you. Spark is fast and Anvil is the tank, with Rivet somewhere between them. You can give each of them a helmet, a chestplate and a weapon.

Level 5 adds Overdrive, which switches on by itself when a boss goes for you. The brothers come out, everyone gets buffs, and Cogwit blocks at least a quarter of every hit, learned or not, at triple the token cost. The fight leaves him drained, and for two minutes afterwards he protects you half as well.

## A few smaller things

- Dying doesn't make him drop. He comes back with you, tells you how far away your stuff is and points a compass at it until you pick it up.
- Get killed by the same kind of mob twice in a row and he'll comment on it.
- He beeps, and every one of those sounds was synthesized for this mod.
- The K menu has a path through the whole mod, with a compass to wherever you need to go next.

## Armor and trophies

There's more in this mod than the companion, and I'd rather say so up front.

### Copper exoframe

An early-game add-on that goes into two accessory slots, over whatever armor you already wear. The buffs are small: faster mining and attacks, a bit more reach, a full-block step, less hunger when you sprint. Wear both pieces and Z shoves mobs away from you.

### Event Horizon

The late-game suit. Each piece is made at a smithing table from its netherite counterpart, a Horizon Template and a Singularity Core, and each does its own thing:

| Piece | What it does |
|---|---|
| Helmet | You see in the dark. With the faceplate down (H) you can breathe underwater, and blindness and darkness don't land |
| Chestplate | A mass meter: a quarter of the damage you take is stored and makes your hits harder. X opens a gravity well that pulls mobs in, and at full mass it becomes Collapse. Arrows near you turn back. Trophy powers only work while you wear it |
| Leggings | Z dashes where you look and keeps your momentum; hits pass through you during the dash |
| Boots | No fall damage, and a fall from 5 blocks or higher lands as a shockwave |
| Leggings and boots | Flight, for 2 tokens a second |
| Full set | Fire and lava can't burn you, and it pulls you back out of the void |

Every piece takes a share off the damage you take. In exchange, the chestplate pulls hostile mobs in from far away, each piece slows you down in water, and the full set sinks.

### Trophies

35 bosses drop them. Four are vanilla (the Ender Dragon, the Wither, the Warden and the Elder Guardian), and the rest come from L_Ender's Cataclysm, Twilight Forest, Bosses'Rise (Block Factory's Bosses), Mowzie's Mobs, The Aether and Iron's Spells 'n Spellbooks. If you don't have one of those mods, its bosses' trophies simply never drop. Anyone who hit the boss during its last 30 seconds gets the trophy. Every trophy has a passive, and all but one also add an active to the ability wheel, which opens while you hold G.

## Getting started

![The crafting recipe for Cogwit]({{recipe}})

1. Craft Cogwit: copper blocks in the corners, amethyst shards on the top and sides, a redstone block in the middle and an eye of ender at the bottom.
2. Put him in the charm slot, or right click while holding him.
3. Give him some copper.
4. Press K and open the Path tab, which walks you through the rest.

## Controls

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| G, hold | Ability wheel |
| K | Menu: path, trophies, agent settings, help and settings |
| Y | Yes when he asks; Shift + Y for always |
| Z | Dash with Horizon leggings, shove with the exoframe |
| X | Gravity well or Collapse, with the Horizon chestplate |
| H | Helmet faceplate |

Scan, mark, brothers, focus, the agent screen, the trophy codex and the panel toggle start unbound. You can reach all of them from the wheel or give them keys under Controls → Cogwit.

## Playing together

Everyone gets their own Cogwit. Hit a boss together and you both get the experience and the trophy. When you stand near a friend, their Cogwit's stronger skills cover you too, and Horizon chestplates close to each other share trophy passives. Mark, on the wheel, drops a marker where you're looking so your friend can find it.

## Requirements

Minecraft 1.21.1 on NeoForge, with Curios API and GeckoLib. It goes on both the client and the server.

A few other mods add extras if you have them. EMI shows the recipes and info pages, and with Create you get a charger that fills token cells from rotation. Sophisticated Backpacks lets him refill your hotbar from backpacks, and Controlify makes the wheel usable on a gamepad.

The mod comes in English and Russian.

## Config

The world's `serverconfig/cogwit-server.toml` holds the balance. `cogwitBlockScale` caps how much his skills block (0.67 means 40%), `armorReduction` and `armorMassReduction` set what the Horizon suit takes off, and `flightTokensPerSecond` is the price of flight.

`config/cogwit-client.toml` covers your own screen: where the panel sits and how much it shows, how much he talks (`chatter`) and whether his lines go to chat as well.

## Questions

**Does he run on AI? He has tokens and he thinks.**
No, nothing in the game calls an AI. His tokens are fuel you get from copper, and his "thinking" is ordinary game logic. He never connects to the internet or to any outside service.

**Can I lose him?**
No, he stays in his slot when you die.

**Forge or Fabric?**
Just NeoForge 1.21.1 for now.

**Does it work in singleplayer?**
Yes, the same as on a server.
